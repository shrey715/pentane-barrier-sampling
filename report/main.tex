\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Molecular simulations are routinely used to explore the conformational
landscape of flexible molecules.  However, a system that evolves according to
physically accurate equations of motion can become indefinitely trapped in a
single local free-energy minimum whenever the energy barriers separating
minima are large compared to the thermal energy $k_{\mathrm{B}}T$.  This
``trapping'' problem is one of the central challenges in computational
statistical mechanics and motivates the development of enhanced sampling
strategies~\citep{Frenkel2002}.

$n$-Pentane (C$_5$H$_{12}$) is a canonical model system for studying
torsional trapping.  Its central C--C--C--C backbone dihedral $\varphi_1$
(defined by atoms C1--C2--C3--C4) partitions conformational space into three
well-known minima: the \emph{trans} state ($\varphi_1 \approx \pm 180^{\circ}$)
and the two \emph{gauche} states ($\varphi_1 \approx \pm 60^{\circ}$).  The
energy barriers between these states are large enough to induce trapping at
low temperature even in simulations of 200\,000 steps.

This report presents a complete Python implementation of:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item An all-atom initial geometry generated from internal coordinates
        (Task~1);
  \item Baseline Metropolis Monte Carlo (MC) and Nos\'{e}--Hoover NVT
        molecular dynamics (MD) simulations at 120\,K and 250\,K
        (Task~2);
  \item Umbrella sampling with Weighted Histogram Analysis Method (WHAM)
        as the enhanced sampling strategy that overcomes the barriers
        (Task~3);
  \item Entropy-based sampling efficiency metrics and potential of mean
        force (PMF) analysis (Task~4).
\end{enumerate}

All simulations use the TraPPE-UA (united-atom) force field of Martin \&
Siepmann~\citeyear{Martin1998}.  No external MD engine is used; every
algorithm is coded from scratch in Python with NumPy, with Numba JIT
compilation used selectively for performance.

\section{Force Field and System Description}
\label{sec:ff}

\subsection{United-Atom Representation}
In the TraPPE-UA model, hydrogen atoms are merged into the attached carbon
centre.  $n$-Pentane is therefore represented as five interaction sites:
two terminal CH$_3$ groups (C1, C5) and three internal CH$_2$ groups
(C2, C3, C4).  This reduces the number of degrees of freedom while
preserving the essential torsional behaviour.

\subsection{Potential Energy Function}
The total potential energy is the sum of four contributions:

\paragraph{Bond stretch.}
\begin{equation}
  U_{\mathrm{bond}} = \sum_{\text{bonds}} \tfrac{1}{2}\, k_r \bigl(r - r_0\bigr)^2
  \label{eq:bond}
\end{equation}
TraPPE-UA specifies \emph{rigid} bonds ($r_0 = 1.54$\,\AA); a stiff harmonic
spring with $k_r = 452{,}900$\,K\,\AA$^{-2}$ (from Mundy et al.\ 1996) is
used here to allow full Cartesian dynamics while maintaining near-rigid bond
lengths.  At equilibrium geometry, the bond stretch contribution to the
total energy is negligible ($\ll 1$\,K).

\paragraph{Angle bend.}
\begin{equation}
  U_{\mathrm{angle}} = \sum_{\text{angles}} \tfrac{1}{2}\, k_\theta
  \bigl(\theta - \theta_0\bigr)^2
  \label{eq:angle}
\end{equation}
with equilibrium angle $\theta_0 = 114.0^{\circ}$ and force constant
$k_\theta = 62{,}500$\,K\,rad$^{-2}$ from Martin \& Siepmann~\citeyear{Martin1998}.
There are three C--C--C angles in $n$-pentane (C1--C2--C3, C2--C3--C4,
C3--C4--C5), all summed over in the implementation.

\paragraph{Torsion.}
The three-term OPLS-style torsional potential:
\begin{equation}
  U_{\mathrm{tors}}(\varphi) = C_0 + C_1(1+\cos\varphi)
    + C_2(1-\cos 2\varphi) + C_3(1+\cos 3\varphi)
  \label{eq:tors}
\end{equation}
with $C_0 = 0$\,K, $C_1 = 355.03$\,K, $C_2 = -68.19$\,K,
$C_3 = 791.32$\,K from Table~2 of Martin \& Siepmann~\citeyear{Martin1998}.
This potential is summed over both backbone dihedrals $\varphi_1$
(C1--C2--C3--C4) and $\varphi_2$ (C2--C3--C4--C5).  The 1--4
non-bonded interactions are absorbed into these torsion coefficients,
so no separate 1--4 LJ terms are added.

\paragraph{Lennard-Jones (1--5 pair only).}
\begin{equation}
  U_{\mathrm{LJ}}(r) = 4\varepsilon\left[\left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{12}
    - \left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{6}\right]
  \label{eq:lj}
\end{equation}
For $n$-pentane, the exclusion rules are: 1--2 (bonded), 1--3
(angle-separated), and 1--4 (dihedral, absorbed in torsion) pairs are all
\emph{excluded}.  The only permitted intramolecular LJ interaction is the
C1$\cdots$C5 (1--5) pair.  Parameters: $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CH}_3}/k_{\mathrm{B}} = 98.0$\,K,
$\sigma_{\mathrm{CH}_3} = 3.75$\,\AA\ (both sites are CH$_3$, so Lorentz--Berthelot
combining rules are trivial).

\paragraph{Torsional barrier height.}
Evaluating Eq.~\eqref{eq:tors} at the cis saddle point ($\varphi = 0$)
gives a barrier of approximately $2{,}292$\,K.  The ratio
$\Delta U / k_{\mathrm{B}}T$ equals 19.1 at 120\,K and 9.2 at 250\,K.
The spontaneous barrier-crossing probability under Metropolis sampling is
therefore $e^{-19.1} \approx 5 \times 10^{-9}$ at 120\,K and
$e^{-9.2} \approx 10^{-4}$ at 250\,K.  For a 200\,000-step simulation
this predicts essentially zero crossings at 120\,K and only $\sim\!20$
at 250\,K --- motivating the enhanced sampling in Task~3.

\section{Task 1: Initial Configuration}
\label{sec:task1}

\subsection{All-Trans Geometry Construction}
The all-trans starting geometry is constructed analytically from internal
coordinates using the standard z-matrix to Cartesian conversion.  Beginning
from C1 at the origin and C2 placed along the positive $x$-axis, each
subsequent atom is placed using \texttt{geometry.place\_atom}, which enforces
the IUPAC dihedral convention ($\varphi = 0$ is cis, $\varphi = \pm\pi$ is
trans).  The resulting five-atom coordinate array has all backbone dihedrals
set exactly to $180^{\circ}$, confirming all-trans initialisation.

The function \texttt{build\_all\_trans(cfg)} reads all bond lengths and
equilibrium angles from the TOML configuration file at runtime.  No
geometry parameters are hard-coded in the Python source.  Self-consistency
of the geometry builder is verified by the round-trip test:
\[
  \texttt{calc\_dihedral(*build\_pentane($\varphi$, $\varphi$, cfg)[[0,1,2,3]])}
  \approx \varphi
\]
to within floating-point tolerance for any input dihedral $\varphi$.

\subsection{Coordinate Convention}
In this work, all simulations propagate full Cartesian coordinates
$(x_i, y_i, z_i)$ for each of the five united-atom sites.  The dihedral
$\varphi_1$ is extracted from the Cartesian positions at every step using
\texttt{geometry.calc\_dihedral}, which computes the signed angle between
the planes defined by C1--C2--C3 and C2--C3--C4 via the cross-product
method:
\begin{equation}
  \varphi_1 = \operatorname{atan2}\!\left(
    \hat{\mathbf{b}}_2 \cdot (\mathbf{n}_1 \times \mathbf{n}_2),\;
    \mathbf{n}_1 \cdot \mathbf{n}_2
  \right)
  \label{eq:dihedral}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{n}_1 = \mathbf{b}_1 \times \mathbf{b}_2$,
$\mathbf{n}_2 = \mathbf{b}_2 \times \mathbf{b}_3$, and
$\mathbf{b}_k$ are the bond vectors.  This gives a result in $(-\pi, \pi]$.

\section{Task 2: Baseline Sampling --- MC and NVT MD}
\label{sec:task2}

\subsection{Monte Carlo Implementation}
The Metropolis--Hastings algorithm~\citep{Metropolis1953} is implemented in
\texttt{src/pentane/mc.py}.  At each step, a single atom is selected
uniformly at random from the five sites.  That atom is displaced by a
uniform random vector $\delta\mathbf{r}$ drawn from
$[-\delta r_{\max}, +\delta r_{\max}]^3$ in Cartesian space, with
$\delta r_{\max} = 0.05$\,\AA.  The trial configuration is accepted with
the standard Metropolis probability:
\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm{acc}} = \min\!\left(1,\; e^{-\beta\,\Delta U}\right),
  \quad \beta = \frac{1}{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}
  \label{eq:metropolis}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta U = U_{\mathrm{trial}} - U_{\mathrm{current}}$ is the full
Cartesian potential energy difference evaluated by \texttt{forcefield.total\_energy}.
The proposal is symmetric in Cartesian space ($T(o \to n) = T(n \to o)$),
so detailed balance reduces exactly to Eq.~\eqref{eq:metropolis}.

\paragraph{Random number pre-generation.}
For computational efficiency, all $n$ atom indices, displacement vectors,
and Metropolis uniform draws are generated in vectorised NumPy calls
\emph{before} the MC loop begins.  This eliminates per-step Python overhead
and achieves approximately $2\times$ speedup relative to in-loop generation.

\paragraph{Step size rationale.}
The displacement limit $\delta r_{\max} = 0.05$\,\AA\ was chosen to give
approximately 40\% acceptance at 120\,K.  This maximises the effective
mean-squared displacement per step,
$\langle \delta^2 \rangle_{\mathrm{eff}} \propto \alpha \cdot \delta r_{\max}^2$,
where $\alpha$ is the acceptance rate.

\subsection{NVT Molecular Dynamics Implementation}
The NVT ensemble is enforced using the Nos\'{e}--Hoover thermostat with the
velocity-Verlet / MTK operator-splitting scheme~\citep{Martyna1996},
implemented in \texttt{src/pentane/md.py}.

\subsubsection{Equations of Motion}
\begin{align}
  \dot{\mathbf{r}}_i &= \frac{\mathbf{p}_i}{m_i} \\
  \dot{\mathbf{p}}_i &= \mathbf{F}_i - \xi\,\mathbf{p}_i \label{eq:nh_p}\\
  \dot{\xi}           &= \frac{1}{Q}\left(\sum_i \frac{p_i^2}{m_i}
                         - N_{\mathrm{dof}}\,k_{\mathrm{B}}T\right) \label{eq:nh_xi}
\end{align}
where the thermostat mass $Q = N_{\mathrm{dof}}\,k_{\mathrm{B}}T\,\tau_T^2$,
$\tau_T = 0.5$\,ps is the coupling time, and $N_{\mathrm{dof}} = 3N - 3 = 12$
(three translational degrees of freedom removed by centre-of-mass fixing).

\subsubsection{Integration Scheme}
The velocity-Verlet integrator with Nos\'{e}--Hoover half-kicks is applied
per step with timestep $dt = 0.002$\,ps:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item First $\xi$ half-kick: $\xi \mathrel{+}= \tfrac{dt}{2}\bigl[(2K - N_{\mathrm{dof}}T)/Q\bigr]$
  \item Half velocity update: $\mathbf{v} \mathrel{+}= \tfrac{dt}{2}\bigl[\mathbf{a} - \xi\mathbf{v}\bigr]$
  \item Full position update: $\mathbf{r} \mathrel{+}= dt\,\mathbf{v}$
  \item Centre-of-mass shift removal
  \item Force evaluation: $\mathbf{F} = -\nabla U(\mathbf{r})$
  \item Half velocity update: $\mathbf{v} \mathrel{+}= \tfrac{dt}{2}\bigl[\mathbf{a} - \xi\mathbf{v}\bigr]$
  \item Second $\xi$ half-kick using kinetic energy \emph{before} COM removal
  \item COM velocity removal
\end{enumerate}
The ordering of steps 7 and 8 follows the correction of Itoh, Morishita \&
Okumura~\citeyear{Itoh2013}: the thermostat must see the kinetic energy
consistent with $N_{\mathrm{dof}} = 12$ (i.e.\ computed before COM velocity
removal), otherwise the thermostat systematically over-damps translational
motion.

\subsubsection{Force Evaluation}
Forces are computed by central finite differences with step $h = 10^{-5}$\,\AA:
\begin{equation}
  F_{i,d} = -\frac{U(\mathbf{r} + h\hat{e}_{i,d})
                  - U(\mathbf{r} - h\hat{e}_{i,d})}{2h}
  \label{eq:fd}
\end{equation}
When the Numba package is available, a JIT-compiled version
(\texttt{forcefield.forces\_numba}) is used instead, giving approximately
$20\times$ speedup over the pure-Python evaluator.

\subsubsection{Initial Conditions}
Velocities are initialised from a Maxwell--Boltzmann distribution at
temperature $T$:
\begin{equation}
  v_{i,d} \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\; \sqrt{T/m_i}\right)
  \label{eq:mb}
\end{equation}
The centre-of-mass velocity is then removed.  Positions are initialised
from the all-trans geometry described in Section~\ref{sec:task1}.

\subsubsection{Temperature Dependence of Barrier Crossing}
The Metropolis crossing probability scales exponentially with
$\beta\,\Delta U$.  At 120\,K, $P_{\mathrm{cross}} \approx 5\times10^{-9}$,
predicting fewer than one crossing in $2\times10^5$ steps.  At 250\,K,
$P_{\mathrm{cross}} \approx 10^{-4}$, predicting $\sim\!20$ crossings ---
consistent with the sparse gauche excursions visible in the MC time series
at 250\,K (Figure~\ref{fig:timeseries}).

MD is more severely trapped than MC at equal temperature because the
continuous displacement per timestep is constrained to
$\dot{\varphi} \cdot dt \sim 0.01^{\circ}$, while MC can apply a direct
Cartesian displacement that maps to a significantly larger dihedral change
per step.  This difference in effective phase-space step size explains
why the 250\,K MC histogram shows more gauche occupancy than the 250\,K
MD histogram (Figure~\ref{fig:basedist}).

\section{Task 3: Enhanced Sampling --- Umbrella Sampling with WHAM}
\label{sec:task3}

\subsection{Motivation}
Both baseline methods fail to produce an ergodic dihedral distribution
because the torsional barrier ($\approx 2{,}292$\,K) is many times larger
than $k_{\mathrm{B}}T$ at the simulated temperatures.  Umbrella
sampling~\citep{Torrie1977} resolves this by artificially biasing the system
to visit all regions of the dihedral space, and then mathematically removing
that bias in post-processing via WHAM~\citep{Kumar1992}.

\subsection{Umbrella Sampling Design}
The full dihedral range $[-\pi, \pi]$ is divided into $N_w = 36$ windows
with centres at the midpoints of 10$^{\circ}$ bins:
\[
  \varphi_0^{(k)} = -175^{\circ} + 10^{\circ}(k-1), \quad k = 1,\ldots, 36.
\]
Within each window $k$, the total potential energy is augmented by a
harmonic bias on the $\varphi_1$ dihedral:
\begin{equation}
  U_{\mathrm{biased}}(\mathbf{r}) = U_{\mathrm{phys}}(\mathbf{r})
    + w_k(\varphi_1),
  \quad
  w_k(\varphi) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,k_b\bigl[\mathrm{wrap}(\varphi - \varphi_0^{(k)})\bigr]^2
  \label{eq:bias}
\end{equation}
with spring constant $k_b = 500$\,K\,rad$^{-2}$.  The $\mathrm{wrap}$
function folds the difference into $(-\pi, \pi]$, correctly handling the
periodicity of the dihedral.  The standard deviation of the window
distribution is $\sigma_w = \sqrt{T/k_b}$, which equals 0.49\,rad
($28^{\circ}$) at 120\,K and 0.71\,rad ($41^{\circ}$) at 250\,K.
This ensures better than 50\% overlap between adjacent windows at both
temperatures, satisfying the WHAM overlap criterion.

\subsection{Within-Window Monte Carlo}
Each window runs a Cartesian single-atom displacement MC identical in
structure to the baseline MC, but with the bias included in the acceptance
criterion.  The trial move displaces one randomly chosen atom by
$\delta\mathbf{r} \in [-0.15, 0.15]^3$\,\AA\ (a larger step than the
baseline 0.05\,\AA\ is used because the harmonic bias already confines
sampling, so larger Cartesian moves help decorrelate $\varphi_1$
within the window faster).  The biased acceptance probability is:
\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm{acc}} = \min\!\left(1,\; e^{-\beta\,\Delta U_{\mathrm{biased}}}\right)
  \label{eq:bias_accept}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta U_{\mathrm{biased}} = \Delta U_{\mathrm{phys}} + \Delta w_k$.
Each window runs for 200\,000 steps and saves the $\varphi_1$ value at
every step.  The 36 windows are run in parallel using
\texttt{concurrent.futures.ProcessPoolExecutor} with each window assigned
an independent RNG seed for reproducibility.

\subsection{WHAM Post-processing}
The $N_w = 36$ biased $\varphi_1$ trajectories are combined using the
Weighted Histogram Analysis Method (WHAM)~\citep{Kumar1992} implemented in
\texttt{src/pentane/wham.py}.

\subsubsection{WHAM Equations}
Bin the combined data into $N_b = 36$ histogram bins with edges at
$\{-\pi + 2\pi j/N_b\}_{j=0}^{N_b}$.  Let $n_k$ be the number of
samples from window $k$, $H_{kj}$ the count of window-$k$ samples
in bin $j$, and $n_{\mathrm{tot},j} = \sum_k H_{kj}$.  The WHAM
self-consistency equations are:
\begin{align}
  \rho_j &= \frac{n_{\mathrm{tot},j}}
    {\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N_w} n_k\,
     \exp\!\bigl[\bigl(f_k - w_k(\varphi_j)\bigr)/T\bigr]}
  \label{eq:wham_rho} \\[4pt]
  f_k    &= -T \ln\!\left[\Delta\varphi\sum_{j=1}^{N_b}
    \rho_j\,e^{-w_k(\varphi_j)/T}\right]
  \label{eq:wham_f}
\end{align}
where $\rho_j$ is the unbiased probability density in bin $j$,
$f_k$ are the per-window free-energy offsets (unknowns to be determined
self-consistently), and $\Delta\varphi = 2\pi/N_b$ is the bin width.

\subsubsection{Iterative Solution}
Starting from $f_k = 0$ for all $k$, Equations~\eqref{eq:wham_rho} and
\eqref{eq:wham_f} are iterated alternately.  $\rho$ is normalised to
unit sum at each step, and $f$ is shifted so $f_1 = 0$ to remove the
free gauge.  Convergence is declared when
$\max_k |f_k^{(\text{new})} - f_k^{(\text{old})}| < 10^{-6}$\,K;
the maximum allowed number of iterations is $10{,}000$.

\subsubsection{PMF Reconstruction}
Once converged, the unbiased PMF is obtained from $\rho_j$ by:
\begin{equation}
  F(\varphi_j) = -T \ln \rho_j + C, \quad
  C = -\min_j(-T\ln\rho_j)
  \label{eq:wham_pmf}
\end{equation}
so that $\min_j F(\varphi_j) = 0$ by convention.

\section{Task 4: Sampling Efficiency and PMF Analysis}
\label{sec:task4}

\subsection{Exploration Entropy}
Following the project specification, the dihedral range is divided into
36 equal bins over $[-\pi, \pi]$.  The exploration entropy at time $t$ is:
\begin{equation}
  S(t) = -\sum_{i=1}^{36} P_i(t)\, \ln P_i(t)
  \label{eq:entropy}
\end{equation}
where $P_i(t)$ is the empirical probability of bin $i$ over the first $t$
steps.  Only bins with non-zero counts contribute to the sum.

\subsection{Early Exploration Score}
The early exploration score is the time-average of the cumulative entropy:
\begin{equation}
  E = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^{N} S(t)
  \label{eq:early}
\end{equation}
where $N = 200{,}000$ is the total number of steps.  A higher $E$
indicates that the simulation discovers new conformations sooner.  For
computational efficiency, the sum is approximated by evaluating $S$ at
$N_c = 500$ linearly spaced checkpoints; the approximation error is $<2.2\%$
relative to the exact sum in validation tests.  At each checkpoint $c$,
the histogram is updated incrementally from the previous checkpoint using
only the new samples in that interval, making the calculation $O(N)$
overall.

\subsection{Potential of Mean Force from Baseline Simulations}
The baseline PMF is obtained by Boltzmann inversion of the normalised
dihedral histogram:
\begin{equation}
  F(\varphi) = -k_{\mathrm{B}}T \ln P(\varphi) + C
  \label{eq:pmf_bl}
\end{equation}
where $P(\varphi)$ is the discrete probability per bin and $C$ is the
shift constant that sets $\min F = 0$.  Bins that received no samples
are assigned NaN and excluded from the profile.

\subsection{PMF Features and Physical Interpretation}
\label{sec:pmf_discussion}

The complete PMF from umbrella/WHAM at both temperatures
(Figures~\ref{fig:whampmf} and \ref{fig:pmfcomp}) resolves all features
of the $n$-pentane torsional free-energy surface:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Trans minimum} at $\varphi_1 = \pm 180^{\circ}$: global
        free-energy minimum; $F = 0$\,K by the shift convention.
        The trans conformation is favoured because it places the terminal
        CH$_3$ groups as far apart as possible, minimising the C1$\cdots$C5
        LJ interaction and the steric repulsion terms encoded in the torsion
        potential.

  \item \textbf{Gauche minima} at $\varphi_1 \approx \pm 60^{\circ}$:
        local minima at $F_{\mathrm{gauche}} \approx 400$--$600$\,K
        ($\sim$3.3--5.0\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$) above the trans state at 120\,K.
        The gauche--trans free-energy difference at 250\,K is smaller than
        at 120\,K, consistent with entropic stabilisation of the gauche
        states at higher temperature.  The published TraPPE-UA gauche--trans
        enthalpy difference is $\sim$2.6\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$; our PMF values
        are slightly higher, partly because they include the LJ and angle
        contributions in the full Cartesian energy.

  \item \textbf{Gauche-to-trans barrier} at $\varphi_1 \approx \pm 120^{\circ}$:
        $F_{\mathrm{barrier}} \approx 1{,}500$--$1{,}800$\,K, the saddle
        point that separates the gauche and trans wells.

  \item \textbf{Cis barrier} at $\varphi_1 = 0^{\circ}$:
        $F_{\mathrm{cis}} \approx 2{,}200$--$2{,}500$\,K, the highest
        point on the free-energy surface.  This corresponds to the eclipsed
        conformation in which both CH$_3$ groups are on the same side of the
        central C--C bond.

  \item \textbf{Temperature dependence}: comparing the 120\,K and 250\,K
        PMFs, the trans--gauche free-energy gap decreases at higher $T$.
        This is a direct manifestation of the $F = U - TS$ decomposition:
        at higher temperatures, entropic contributions weight the shallower
        gauche wells more heavily relative to the deeper trans well.
\end{itemize}

The PMF comparison panels (Figure~\ref{fig:pmfcomp}) show that in the
limited region sampled by the baseline methods (near the trans minimum),
the Boltzmann-inversion PMF from MC at 250\,K agrees with the WHAM PMF
to within the statistical noise of the histogram.  This cross-check
validates the WHAM implementation: in the well-sampled region, both
methods recover the same free-energy profile.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why Baseline Methods Fail}
The failure of baseline MC and MD at 120\,K is thermodynamically
inevitable, not a coding error.  The Landau free-energy barrier exceeds
$5\,k_{\mathrm{B}}T$ at 250\,K and $15\,k_{\mathrm{B}}T$ at 120\,K;
spontaneous fluctuations of this magnitude are exponentially suppressed.
The dihedral time series in Figure~\ref{fig:timeseries} and the entropy
statistics in Table~\ref{tab:efficiency} constitute direct numerical
evidence of trapping.

MD is more severely trapped than MC for a subtle reason.  In MD,
the dihedral $\varphi_1$ changes continuously by small amounts each
timestep ($\sim$0.01$^{\circ}$ per step); to cross the barrier the system
must accumulate a coherent fluctuation of $\sim$60$^{\circ}$ over many
correlated steps.  MC, by contrast, proposes discrete Cartesian
displacements; although each individual move is small, the absence of
inertia means the system is not required to ``carry momentum through''
the barrier, and lucky sequences of moves occasionally generate gauche
excursions at 250\,K.

\subsection{Why Umbrella Sampling Succeeds}
Umbrella sampling succeeds because it \emph{changes the sampling problem}
rather than relying on rare-event statistics.  By partitioning dihedral
space into 36 small windows and forcing the system to remain near each
$\varphi_0$, every region of the PMF is sampled adequately regardless
of its free energy.  The WHAM equations then provide an unbiased estimator
of the true equilibrium distribution by exactly accounting for the known
bias potential $w_k(\varphi)$.  The key requirements for this to work
correctly are: (i) sufficient window overlap, ensured here by $k_b = 500$\,K\,rad$^{-2}$
and 10$^{\circ}$ spacing; and (ii) WHAM convergence, verified by
Figure~\ref{fig:whamconv}.

\subsection{Consistency between WHAM and Baseline PMF}
In the trans region ($\varphi_1 \approx \pm 130^{\circ}$--$180^{\circ}$),
where the baseline MC at 250\,K gathered sufficient statistics, the
Boltzmann-inversion PMF and the WHAM PMF agree within statistical noise
(right panels of Figure~\ref{fig:pmfcomp}).  This provides a non-trivial
internal consistency check: the two completely independent methods ---
unbiased sampling with histogram inversion vs.\ biased umbrella windows
with WHAM debiasing --- agree wherever direct comparison is possible.

\subsection{TraPPE-UA Fidelity}
All torsion, LJ, and angle parameters match Martin \& Siepmann~\citeyear{Martin1998}
exactly (Table~\ref{tab:params}).  The harmonic bond-stretch potential is
not in the original TraPPE-UA specification (which uses rigid bonds), but
the stiff spring constant $k_r = 452{,}900$\,K\,\AA$^{-2}$ from Mundy et
al.\ 1996 contributes negligible energy at equilibrium geometry and allows
the simulation to use a unified Cartesian framework for both MC and MD.
The 1--4 exclusion and 1--5 inclusion follow the published rules exactly.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This project has demonstrated the conformational trapping problem in
$n$-pentane and resolved it using umbrella sampling with WHAM
post-processing.  The main findings are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Task 1 --- Geometry}: An all-trans initial geometry for
        $n$-pentane was generated analytically from internal coordinates
        using the TraPPE-UA bond length (1.54\,\AA) and equilibrium angle
        ($114.0^{\circ}$), and verified by round-trip dihedral checks.

  \item \textbf{Task 2 --- Baseline sampling}: Both MC and NVT MD simulations
        at 120\,K and 250\,K confirm trapping: exploration entropy reaches
        only 34--65\% of the maximum, barrier crossings are exponentially
        rare, and the baseline PMF covers only the trans well.  MD is more
        severely trapped than MC owing to its continuous, inertia-driven
        dynamics.

  \item \textbf{Task 3 --- Enhanced sampling}: Umbrella sampling with 36
        windows, $k_b = 500$\,K\,rad$^{-2}$, and 200\,000 steps per window
        successfully forces the system to visit all 36 dihedral bins at
        both 120\,K and 250\,K. WHAM converges within 90 iterations at
        both temperatures and reconstructs a smooth, complete PMF over
        $[-180^{\circ}, 180^{\circ}]$.

  \item \textbf{Task 4 --- PMF and Efficiency analysis}: The WHAM PMF
        resolves the trans global minimum ($F = 0$), gauche local minima
        ($\approx$400--600\,K above trans), gauche-to-trans barriers
        ($\approx$1\,600\,K), and the cis maximum ($\approx$2\,300\,K).
        In the trans region, the Boltzmann-inversion PMF from 250\,K MC
        agrees quantitatively with WHAM.  The exploration metrics show
        that while umbrella sampling successfully visits 36/36 bins, its
        pooled exploration entropy is $\approx 2.36$--$2.70$\,nats. The soft harmonic
        bias permits local skewing toward the minima within windows, but the
        early exploration score quantifies the vast superiority of umbrella
        sampling over baseline trapping.
\end{enumerate}
